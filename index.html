I

The Age of Gods

What the world was before the fog — the weave, the tree, and the powers that tended them.

Existence was once held together by the Web of Fate — a living weave that bound the past, present, and future destinies of every soul into a single order. It was tended by a silent deity the world remembers only as the Fate Goddess, whose story is knotted into Yggdrasil, the world-tree.

From that bond between goddess and tree came the Woodlings — the small bark-and-branch folk who still scavenge the ruins. Their oldest tellers insist they and the Goddess are "born of the same root," a claim the camp's lore-keeper, the white crow Soekja, quietly confirms.

The Fate Goddess was not alone. The pantheon held names now spoken only in curses and prayers: Kjernnos, whose blood would prove the most corrosive of all; Argus, remembered for teaching mortals lightning, and for the meteor-fall omen called the Decree of Argus; and Velvar, under whose "silent gaze" mortals are still said to find the courage to go on. Against them all stood the Outer Gods — unknowable powers from beyond the weave.

II

The Mistfall

How a war in heaven ended the world — the single catastrophe every questline circles back to.

The War

Gods against Outer Gods

The pantheon went to war with the Outer Gods. It did not end in victory. It ended in mutual annihilation — every deity, on both sides, fell.

The Blood

The Gyldenmist rises

Divine blood soaked into the land and congealed into the Gyldenmist: a golden, corrosive fog that warps souls, drives the living to madness, and mutates them into the shambling Corroded.

The Break

The Web of Fate shatters

With its keepers dead, the weave came undone. Destiny itself frayed — souls could no longer find their way home, and the dead began to pile up in the fog, unable to return. This unravelling is the Mistfall.

The Ember

A splinter survives

One fragment of the Fate Goddess's soul endured the war. It was reborn into the mortal ruins as a maiden — Dew — carrying just enough of the weave to begin mending it.

IV

The Camp & Its Keepers

Between runs, hunters return to a single safe harbor. Its residents are how the game feeds you the lore — one conversation at a time.

Dew

Maiden of Fate · Reviver

The fragment of the Fate Goddess. She grants fallen heroes immortal flesh, reads the Web through the camp's Goddess Statue and Gyldenblod Pool, and points the hunters at each hidden threat. Your guide, and the story's voice.

Soekja

The White Crow · Lore-keeper

A great pale raven at the docks who recovers the gear of hunters who fall. Keeper of the old stories — it is Soekja who recounts the bond between the Fate Goddess and Yggdrasil.

Dramm

Spirit Blacksmith

Forges for the camp while he waits — ringing the Bell of Return over and over, trying to call his lost sister home. He does not yet understand why she will not come.

Drífa

Dramm's Sister

A melancholic child who remembers Brandrgarde too well. She carries the truth her brother can't face: the monster in the mist was their father.

Pip & Merimo

Woodling Merchant & Caravan

Pip is the cunning little Woodling who runs the Auction House; Merimo, his enormous goat, keeps the caravan rolling between camp and the fog.

Cazarro

Trader · Philosopher

The camp's dealer in materials, forever musing on wealth and on what Gyldenblod really is. His "Path to Prosperity" is half commerce, half sermon.

Sigrid

Tavern Brewmaster

Keeps the hearth and the cups. She readies hunters for what waits in the mist — and pours something strong for the ones who make it back.

Twig

Woodling Scout · Scavenger Squad

A curious little scout who first sets you toward Soekja, baffled that anyone would bow to a divine statue. His squad suspects the Woodlings' origin is bound to the Goddess herself.

V

The Griefs of Weavereach

The mainline story isn't about the war — it's about the small, human wreckage left in its fog. These are the arcs to build the video's heart on.

Arc · The Blacksmith, the Sister, the Father

Not Even Death Can Part Us

Dramm rings the Bell of Return to bring his sister Drífa home from the fog. The bell sounds — and fades to silence. She never arrives.

“The chime rises and fades, until at last it falls silent.”

Quest — Siblings

Dew finds the reason: Drífa's soul is snared in Brandrgarde by "a vast and sinister soul, hidden within the Gyldenmist" — a presence that binds the lost "like tangled roots" and keeps them from the Web. That presence is Otto, once a royal researcher of the Capital, whom a mining collapse and starvation drove mad. Steeped in the blood of the god Kjernnos, he became Otto, the Flesheater. To free the daughter, the hunter must kill the father.

“Now, his wails of grief and fury have finally been silenced.”

Quest — The Flesheater's Feast
Arc · The Sin That Started It

The Soul Experiments & the Fate Mocker

Beneath Brandrgarde, the Varg clan tried to seize the authority of dead gods. They sought to "piece countless souls into a single vessel" — a new life form built to hold a myriad of spirits at once. It failed horribly; the crammed souls "never truly became one, continuously collapsing in agony and madness."

After you put down the corrupted general Harald, Dew senses the true hand behind it all — "someone remains hidden deep within the shadows, pulling the strings of countless souls and fates." It is a long-lost priestess, the Fate Mocker, trapped in Hallowgrove between endless death and endless waking, bound to the experiments. She waits at the end as Sálmar, the Cursed Moonwane.

“Once mortals coveted the authority of the divine, all attempts turned into calamity.”

Quest — Soul Experiment
VI

The Mist Lords

The Gyldenmist's greatest horrors — each one a person the fog hollowed out. Beating them is how the weave gets mended.

General Harald

The Golden Berserker

The fallen general of Brandrgarde, corrupted into a Mist Lord — a berserker swinging a golden greataxe, golden threads of stolen fate drifting from his form.

Otto, the Flesheater

Victim of Kjernnos's Blood

A scholar remade into a flesh-devouring monstrosity by Gyldenization. His soul knots the lost spirits of Brandrgarde in place — Drífa's among them.

Sálmar, the Cursed Moonwane

The Fate Mocker

The lost priestess, the season's true antagonist — imprisoned in Hallowgrove's Divine Anchor, endlessly dying, pulling the strings of souls and fates from the dark.

Also stalking the ruins — elite Corroded: Brandrgarde Captain · Foul Hound Alpha · Fungus Matron

VII

Season 1 — Soul Hunt

The living storyline players are unravelling right now.

The Varg clan's forbidden work did not die with Brandrgarde. Their real ambition is monstrous: to weave the harvested dead into Cocoon Puppets and, from countless stolen souls, assemble a False God — a counterfeit deity whose birth would tear the already-broken Web of Fate fully apart.

Against that, the hunters' loop is the whole cosmology in miniature: descend into the Gyldenmist, harvest Gyldenblod from the corrupted, slay a Returner Woodling for its Soul of Return to escape — and carry that golden blood back to Dew, one thread at a time, to re-weave a world that should have ended.
